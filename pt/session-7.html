Sessão 7

Environmental and climate studies: fire, weather and hazards

Quarta-feira, 9 de dezembro · 09:30 – 10:30
  • 09:30
    Calibrating a Burned Area Index for the Wide Field Imager (WFI) onboard Brazilian satellites
    Yasmin Sakadauskas Ferreira (UFSCar), Alisson Cleiton de Oliveira (INPE), Thales Sehn Körting (INPE)
    Resumo

    Fires of anthropogenic origin are recurrent in Brazil and strongly affect the Cerrado biome. This work addresses burned area mapping in the Cerrado with the Wide Field Imager (WFI) camera family of the CBERS-4, CBERS4A, and AMAZONIA-1 satellites, which lacks a shortwave infrared (SWIR) band. The reference values of the Burned Area Index (BAI), originally calibrated for Mediterranean ecosystems, were recalibrated for the red and near-infrared (NIR) bands through an exhaustive search over seven CBERS-4/AWFI scenes, using reference masks obtained by manual vectorization of burned areas. The convergence point shifted from red = 0.10 and NIR = 0.06 to red = 0.087 and NIR = 0.076. The calibrated BAI reduced omission errors and raised the F1 score from 0.48 to 0.59 and the Intersection over Union (IoU) from 0.32 to 0.42, at the cost of a slight increase in commission errors, concentrated over water bodies and drainage networks. The calirated BAI outperforms the original one for burned area mapping in the Cerrado, although operational use still requires machine-learning classifiers and time series

    A GeoAI Framework for Monthly Wind Speed Prediction over the Paraná Basin
    João Eliakin Oliveira (Itaipu Parquetec), Geronimo Lemos (INPE), Marilia Shimizu (INPE), Gilvan Sampaio (INPE)
    Resumo

    This study applies Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (GeoAI) to one-month-ahead prediction of 10 m wind speed over the Paraná Basin using gridded ERA5 reanalysis data. Monthly climatology, persistence, Ridge, XGBoost, and U-Net models were evaluated using MAE and skill relative to climatology. Results show strong spatial and seasonal variability in model performance, with the U-Net providing the most consistent improvements in selected months and regions. The findings highlight the value of spatially explicit evaluation and support adaptive GeoAI strategies for regional wind prediction
