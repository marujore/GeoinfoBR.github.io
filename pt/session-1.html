Remote sensing image analysis: land use and forests

Segunda-feira, 7 de dezembro · 11:00 – 12:00
  • 11:00
    Analysis of land use and land cover class conversion for mining tailings in Mariana (MG) using Random Forest classification and Landsat 8 images
    Andressa Penha (Unicamp), Manoel Rodrigues Neto (Unicamp)
    Resumo

    This study had for objective the mapping of land use and land cover for the years of 2015 and 2016 in the region affected by the rupture of the dam of Fundão in Mariana (MG). Satellite imagery from Landsat 8 was used, processed and classified by Random Forest algorithm for mapping, as well as validation methods for the generated model, analysis tools for the results obtained and evaluation metrics. The results demonstrated that the models were robust, the values obtained in Kappa index were almost perfect and the values obtained in F1-Score indicated high classification performance. The iron oxide index highlighted areas affected by the rupture of the dam in the generated maps, indicating the efficiency of the classification by Random Forest.

    Multimodal Analysis and Spatial Generalization for Aboveground Biomass Estimation in Secondary Forests
    Roberto Paiva (UEG), Manuel Ferreira (UFG)
    Resumo

    Integrating different remote sensing data with machine learning can extend the capacity to estimate aboveground biomass (AGB) over large areas. Nevertheless, it is still necessary to understand which information effectively contributes to the models and whether the gains obtained hold in new regions. In this study, we used 460 sample units of the Brazilian National Forest Inventory (IFN-Brasil) distributed across the Amazon and the Cerrado, together with multimodal remote sensing information, to assess these questions. The ablation and interpretation experiments have shown that the modalities contribute unequally: structural variables concentrated most of the predictive information, and the combination of structural data with SAR data performed close to the complete set. However, the gain of the multimodal model relative to a linear model based on canopy height alone was small (R2 OOF = 0.427 and 0.391, respectively). Moreover, this gain did not hold when biomes or states were excluded from training, a setting in which the lower-complexity model was superior in most comparisons. The results indicate that multimodal integration can add information to AGB estimation, but that greater variable diversity and model complexity do not ensure spatial transferability.

    Multimodal Learning for Selective Logging Detection via Fusion of Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 Data
    Mariana Santos (UnB), Pedro Freitas (UnB)
    Resumo

    Automated monitoring of selective logging in the Brazilian Legal Amazon remains challenging because logging produces subtle, spatially heterogeneous disturbances that are difficult to distinguish from natural canopy dynamics. Although the integration of Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and optical imagery is physically motivated, the relative effectiveness of different deep learning paradigms for this task is still poorly understood. This study presents a multimodal benchmark for patch-level detection of disordered selective logging using Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical data. We construct an early-fusion dataset based on DETER alerts, apply hard negative mining, preserve fine spatial resolution through architectural adaptation, and enforce a polygon-based spatial split to reduce data leakage. Twelve models spanning convolutional neural networks (CNNs), Vision Transformers (ViTs), State Space Models (SSMs), and self-supervised foundation models are evaluated under a common protocol. The multimodal EfficientNet-B0 obtains the best F1-score (0.9314) and an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.9800, while ShuffleNet attains the highest overall AUC among the evaluated models (0.9842). The results indicate that, for 64 × 64 remote-sensing patches, lightweight CNNs and SSM-based models can outperform ViTs and larger foundation models, offering a favorable accuracy efficiency trade-off for operational environmental monitoring.
