Geospatial software, plugins and web platforms

Wednesday, December 9 · 08:00 – 09:10
  • 08:00
    Redesign and Architectural Modernization of a Municipal Cadastral Web GIS for Non-Expert Users
    Robon Silva (UNIFEI), Vanessa Souza (UNIFEI), Bruno Batista (UNIFEI)
    Abstract

    Web Geographic Information Systems (Web GIS) face persistent challenges regarding user interaction, cartographic visualization, and technical performance. This study investigates the combined effects of user interface redesign, geovisualization enhancements, and architectural decoupling in a municipal cadastral Web GIS. System modernization was guided by User Experience (UX) principles and a decoupled front-end architecture. Evaluation involved objective technical audits via Lighthouse and an exploratory user perception survey using an adapted QUIS instrument. Lighthouse scores demonstrated substantial gains in performance (66 to 86), accessibility (100), and SEO (100). The exploratory evaluation with 26 participants revealed strong user acceptance (overall mean score of 4.68/5.0) and high spatial task success rates (up to 92%). Qualitative feedback highlighted the need for improved cartographic contrast and simplified cadastral terminology. The findings demonstrate measurable technical gains and high acceptance of the redesigned application among non-expert users, providing evidence on the needs of citizens interacting with cadastral Web GIS and suggesting that coordinated design and architectural interventions are a viable strategy for this class of applications.

  • 08:15
    Bringing Google Earth Engine to Decision-Makers: A Web Architecture Applied to Water-Use Regulation
    Luiz Lustosa Pascoal (UFG), Hugo do Nascimento Bendini (UNESP), Leila Maria Garcia Fonseca (INPE), Caio Augusto Bertolini (INPE), Daniel Assumpção Costa Ferreira (ANA), Tharles de Sousa Andrade (UFG), Thiago Henriques Fontenelle (ANA), Ravi Fernandes Mariano (INPE), Morris Scherer-Warren (ANA), Renato Gomes Silvério (UFG), Bruno Collischonn (ANA), Pedro Lacerda Santos (INPE), Wagner Silva (ANA)
    Abstract

    Brazil's National Water Agency (ANA) requires satellite evidence to manage irrigation permits, yet Google Earth Engine (GEE) reaches its analysts only through scripts. This paper presents the software architecture of Sat-Irriga and ESPIA, two ANA–INPE web platforms where a single Python job service is the only gateway to GEE. Index curves and map tiles are returned immediately, irrigated-area mapping runs as queued jobs and daily pivot alerts run as scheduled exports, under one REST job contract explained by an analytical cost model. Analysts compare method results visually against imagery and reference layers before approving them. Sat-Irriga monitors 2.45 million hectares and ESPIA 437 pivots daily.
