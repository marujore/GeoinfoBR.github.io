    Topological Characteristics of the Street Network of São Paulo City, Brazil
    Pedro Augusto Borges dos Santos (National Institute for Space Research), Leonardo Bacelar Lima Santos (National Institute for Space Research)
    Abstract

    We analyze the topological characteristics of the street network of São Paulo City, Brazil, modeled from OpenStreetMap as a directed multigraph with 122,456 nodes and 304,027 edges. Node degree (k_i), clustering coefficient (c_i), average shortest path length (l_i) and edge betweenness centrality (e_{ij}) were computed and compared with a directed configuration model preserving the degree sequences. The degree distribution is not scale-free and the most common degree is 6; a small subset of edges concentrates the highest e_{ij} values, marking critical segments. Against the random graph, the real network has an average c_i three orders of magnitude higher and an average l_i 12 times longer, reflecting the geographical constraints of street infrastructure.

    Análise espaço-temporal e reprodutível da regularidade operacional do BRT do Rio de Janeiro a partir de dados GPS
    Ariel cantarino (Ime)
    Abstract

    Este estudo analisa a regularidade operacional do sistema BRT do Rio de Janeiro por meio de registros de posicionamento veicular obtidos em 22 de setembro de 2026. A partir de 114.776 posições válidas, referentes a 611 veículos e 33 linhas, foram reconstruídas 2.160 instâncias de viagem, das quais 991 atenderam aos critérios de completude estabelecidos. A análise identificou 1.382 partidas e 1.317 intervalos consecutivos, permitindo examinar a dispersão dos intervalos e a variabilidade dos tempos de percurso. O coeficiente de variação mediano dos intervalos foi de 0,560, enquanto o dos tempos de percurso foi de 0,077. Os resultados demonstram o potencial dos dados GPS para caracterizar a operação observada, embora as estimativas dependam da cobertura temporal e dos critérios de inferência espacial adotados.

    Fraseia Aqui: A Brazilian Portuguese Phrase Geocoder on an Equal-Area Discrete Global Grid System
    Israel Silva (Instituto Tecnológico de Aeronáutica), Gabriel Dietzsch (Instituto de Estudos Avançados), Elcio Shiguemori (Instituto de Estudos Avançados)
    Abstract

    Fraseia Aqui is a phrase-based geocoder for Brazil. Each 5 m cell in its land and coastal-marine coverage receives a four-word Brazilian Portuguese phrase (noun, adjective, verb, adverb) on the equal-area ITACaRT grid. A seeded Feistel permutation assigns verb–adverb pairs to 144,578 coarse 10 km cells, while noun–adjective pairs select fine cells along a continuous curve. The codec is a verified bijection, with a lexicon curated to minimize confusion. For one-word errors, confusable noun swaps move locations by a median of 6 m; about 42% of uniform verb or adverb swaps are rejected; and confusable verb or adverb swaps that decode move locations by a median of about 1,900 km and never less than 20 km.

    Multi-Document Question Answering Approach for the Queimadas Program Based on LLMs, RAG, and XAI
    Maria Cruz (INPE), Valdivino Júnior (INPE)
    Abstract

    The Brazilian National Institute for Space Research (INPE) publishes monthly InfoQueima bulletins with information on fire hotspots and burned areas. Answering questions that require information from this collection involves locating and consolidating evidence across technical documents. This study presents QAFire, a multi-document question answering (QA) approach based on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for the twelve InfoQueima bulletins published in 2024. The pipeline combines dense and lexical retrieval, month-specific indexes, geographical routing, question-type filtering, and Cross-Encoder reranking. A dataset of 1,102 question-answer records was automatically generated from official table values using Python and pandas, then reviewed to remove ambiguous cases. Five Large Language Models (LLMs) were evaluated on the same document collection and dataset, without fine-tuning. Qwen3-4B achieved 90.56% precision, 94.53% recall, and a mean per-question F1-score of 90.54%, while Llama-3.1-8B achieved 93.19% precision and the lowest field-level hallucination rate (2.48%). The revised hallucination measure adapts RAGAS Faithfulness to the task's structured factual fields: it checks the month, geographical location, and hotspot count stated in an answer against the values reported in the official tables. Segment-level SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were also used to analyze the question and seven retrieved passages for Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B. The results demonstrate how multi-document retrieval, factual evaluation, and explainability can be combined in a QA approach for environmental information.
