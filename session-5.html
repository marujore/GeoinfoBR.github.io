Urban analytics and spatial inequality

Tuesday, December 8 · 13:00 – 14:30
  • 13:00
    Beyond Homogeneity: A Multidimensional Clustering Framework for Spatial Typologies in Favelas
    Gabriel Marques da Silva (CEFAVELA), Heitor Guimarães (INPE), Adirson Freitas Junior (CEFAVELA)
    Abstract

    Informal settlements are highly heterogeneous territories. This study applies unsupervised machine learning (K-Means, K-Medoids, and GMM) to identify socio-spatial typologies in Paraisópolis, Brazil. By clustering census and morphological variables across four independent dimensions (infrastructure, morphology, demography, and socioeconomics), the methodology prevents the masking of cross-vulnerabilities. The synthesis into five Super-Typologies reveals complex internal dynamics, such as commercial centralities and rapid verticalization driven by the informal rental market. This multidimensional framework provides robust, data-driven insights to support targeted urban upgrading and public housing policies.

  • 13:15
    Analysis of Population Density and Vegetation in the São Paulo Metropolitan Region
    Bruno Perciani (UFABC), Victor Fernandez Nascimento (UFABC), Angela Terumi Fushita (UFABC)
    Abstract

    Vegetation mitigates urban environmental challenges, particularly in expanding megacities affected by climate change. This study assesses average NDVI and Land Use/Cover evolution in the São Paulo Metropolitan Region (SPMR, 2000–2022), examines the NDVI–population density (PD) relationship, and identifies green urban infrastructure priority areas. Results reveal a non linear NDVI–PD relationship, significant only in low-density areas (PD < 47 in hab/ha), and high-priority zones across central and peripheral municipalities, supporting targeted green-infrastructure policy.

  • 13:30
    Evaluating Geospatial Transfer Learning for Urban Social Vulnerability Estimation from Sentinel-2 Imagery
    Nathalia Farinha Rodrigues (CPqD), Fernando Nascimento (Bowdoin College), Jeova Farias Sales Rocha Neto (Bowdoin College), Ademar Takeo Akabane (PUC-Campinas)
    Abstract

    Estimating urban social vulnerability from satellite imagery remains challenging due to spectral heterogeneity, socioeconomic class imbalance, and limited spatial generalization. This study investigates the effectiveness of geospatial transfer learning for census-tract-level vulnerability estimation using Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery. We compare ResNet18 configurations initialized with ImageNet and TorchGeo pretrained representations, incorporating data augmentation and class-balancing strategies. Experiments involve 2,389 census tracts in Campinas, Brazil, categorized into six ordinal vulnerability levels according to the São Paulo Social Vulnerability Index (IPVS 2022). Performance is evaluated using stratified validation and a geographically held-out district, with metrics accounting for class imbalance and ordinal agreement. On stratified validation, the TorchGeo-based configuration improved Balanced Accuracy from 37.85% to 48.30%, Macro-F1 from 0.3811 to 0.4613, and Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) from 0.5541 to 0.6272. In the held-out district, it achieved 41.51% Balanced Accuracy and 0.5625 QWK, outperforming the ImageNet-based baseline on these metrics. These findings highlight the potential of combining geospatial pretrained representations with regularization strategies to improve ordinal vulnerability estimation, while revealing challenges in geographic generalization. The study provides an empirical foundation for scalable, satellite-assisted urban vulnerability assessment.

  • 13:45
    Walkability-adjusted accessibility and the 15-minute city in Belo Horizonte
    Beatriz Barbosa (UFMG), Clodoveu Davis Junior (UFMG)
    Abstract

    The 15-minute city model proposes that all essential urban functions to sustain a decent urban life should be accessible to every citizen in 15 minutes through sustainable means of transportation, such as walking or biking. The application of this model is, however, still primarily concentrated in countries of the Global North and anchored in proximity-based approaches. This study proposes a walkability-based model to map accessibility to essential urban functions in the city of Belo Horizonte, modeling the city as a walkability-weighted graph using OpenStreetMap and OpenTopography data. The results reveal severe accessibility disparities between city regions, deepened mainly by proximity to commercial points of interest.
